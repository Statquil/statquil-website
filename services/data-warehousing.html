Practice 01

Data warehousing

A warehouse is only useful if people trust the number they see. We design, build and migrate warehouses and lakehouses that reconcile, perform and stay understandable as the business changes.

01 / The work

A warehouse people can defend.

Trust is an engineering outcome. It comes from a model that reconciles, pipelines that fail visibly and definitions that mean the same thing in every report.

We begin with the decisions the warehouse has to support, then work backwards into models, pipelines and controls. The result is a platform with fewer moving parts and a clear line from source system to reported number.

  • Reference architecture and data model, chosen for the operating reality: Kimball, Inmon, Data Vault or a deliberate hybrid
  • Batch, CDC and streaming ingestion with schema contracts, replay and late arriving data handling
  • Layered transformations with tested business logic and clear ownership at every layer
  • Semantic layer and governed metric definitions that stay consistent across tools
  • Legacy migration with parallel runs, reconciliation and source by source sign off
  • Performance, partitioning, clustering and warehouse sizing based on measured queries
  • Data quality checks, lineage and freshness monitoring wired into operations
  • Documentation, runbooks and handover to the team that will own the platform

02 / Delivery

How we deliver.

  1. 01

    Model the business

    We start with the reported numbers and the decisions behind them, then work back to grains, keys and history. The model follows the business, not the source schema.

  2. 02

    Build for failure

    Pipelines carry schema contracts, replay, freshness checks and alerting. A failed job should be visible before a person notices a missing number.

  3. 03

    Reconcile before cutover

    Legacy and new run in parallel. Row counts, sums and sample trails are signed off before a source system is switched off.

  4. 04

    Tune with evidence

    Partitioning, clustering and warehouse sizing follow measured query patterns. Cost is reviewed against the workload that caused it.

04 / Questions

What clients ask first.

Do you work with our existing warehouse or start fresh?

Both are possible. If the current platform is sound, we fix the model, pipelines and controls around it. If it is structurally wrong for the workload, we will say so and show the cost of keeping it. A rebuild is recommended only when the numbers support it.

How do you handle data quality?

Quality is built into the pipeline, not reported after the fact. We define tests at the source, transformation and metric layers, with thresholds, quarantine paths and an owner for every failure. Reconciliation against the source system is part of acceptance.

Can you migrate without downtime?

In most cases, yes. We use parallel runs, change data capture and a controlled cutover window. The business keeps reporting from the old platform until the new one reconciles and is signed off.

Who owns the models and code?

You do. Everything we build is in your repositories, under your cloud accounts, with your credentials. Handover includes architecture records, tests, runbooks and a working session with your team.

How is a warehouse engagement priced?

Fixed scope for a defined build or migration, or a monthly fee for an embedded team. Assessments are short and separately priced, so you can decide before committing to a larger program.
